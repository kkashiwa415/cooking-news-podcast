Bill Gates' daughter's startup is facing serious allegations about how it actually makes its money.

Phia, the shopping app founded by Phoebe Gates and her co-founder Sophia Kianni, has been accused of a deceptive practice called cookie stuffing. Here's what that means: when you're shopping online and click through a link to make a purchase, the company that referred you gets an affiliate commission. Cookie stuffing is when a company secretly plants cookies on your browser without your knowledge or consent, then claims credit for purchases you make later—even though they never actually helped you find or buy anything. It's essentially fraud on a massive scale in the affiliate marketing world.

A major investigation uncovered that Phia has been doing exactly this, which means the startup received commissions and credit for sales it had absolutely nothing to do with. This is particularly problematic because Phia's entire business model relies on affiliate partnerships and earning those commissions legitimately. When you're supposed to be connecting shoppers with retailers they want to buy from, secretly taking credit for purchases you didn't drive is a massive breach of trust with the retailers and marketers you're partnering with.

The fallout here affects not just Phia's reputation, but the broader ecosystem of affiliate marketers and shopping apps that actually play by the rules. Retailers and brands now have to wonder if they can trust any of these referral services. For Phia specifically, this investigation could spark regulatory scrutiny, pressure from partners, and serious damage to the credibility it needs to survive as a shopping platform. The startup was built on the premise that it saves users money and gets them deals, but if it's gaming the system to boost its own numbers, that founding principle looks pretty hollow.

Going forward, watch for whether Phia faces regulatory action or lawsuits from the retailers who unknowingly paid commissions for sales they would've made anyway. You should also watch whether other shopping apps and affiliate platforms get dragged into similar investigations.

You see, when the pandemic hit, Pfizer became a household name and its stock surged. But as the world moved past the acute crisis phase, vaccine demand normalized and the company faced a tough reality—it needed to prove it could still be a healthy business without pandemic-era revenues. That turnaround is actually already happening. The company is refocusing on its core therapeutic areas, managing costs more efficiently, and its pipeline of new drugs is showing real promise. For investors who might have written Pfizer off as a pandemic story, the company is demonstrating it's got real staying power and growth potential beyond vaccines.

Watch how their product lines and marketing shift to support this positioning.
