Now let's talk about an unexpected side effect of one climate solution that's getting massive corporate investment. Google, Microsoft, and other major tech companies have committed hundreds of millions of dollars to carbon removal startups that use a process called enhanced weathering. The idea is to spread crushed basalt, olivine, or other alkaline rock materials across farmland to capture carbon from the atmosphere and lock it away. It's elegant in theory, but researchers have found a potential problem: using rocks for carbon removal could eventually reduce crop growth. The mechanism is complicated, but essentially, changing soil chemistry at scale could have unintended consequences for agriculture. This matters because a huge portion of these carbon removal initiatives depend on farmland, the same land we use to grow food. If the cure for climate change inadvertently undermines food production, that's a problem we need to solve before we scale this technology globally. For the companies investing in this, it means more research is needed before they can confidently market their solutions. For farmers considering participating in these programs, it means asking harder questions about long term soil health impacts. The outlook here is that we're going to see a lot more scrutiny on these kinds of climate tech interventions to make sure the solution doesn't create a different crisis.
