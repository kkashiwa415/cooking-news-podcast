There's also a technical threat that's been getting attention in the crypto space. Bitcoin has a quantum computing problem that Ethereum doesn't. The quantum clock is ticking, and Bitcoin's vulnerability is becoming more urgent. Here's the issue. Bitcoin's public key cryptography could theoretically be broken by sufficiently advanced quantum computers. Once quantum computers are powerful enough, someone could potentially derive private keys from public addresses. Ethereum has some advantages in this regard because it already uses some quantum-resistant techniques and its architecture makes quantum attacks harder to execute. Bitcoin doesn't have those protections built in. This isn't an immediate threat. Quantum computers of that capability don't exist yet. But the timeline is uncertain. It could be years or decades. Bitcoin's community knows about this problem, but there's been less urgency around solving it compared to Ethereum. As quantum computing advances, this becomes more pressing. Watch to see if Bitcoin's developers accelerate work on quantum-resistant solutions or if this becomes a major liability. The cryptocurrency that solves this problem first gains a significant trust advantage.
