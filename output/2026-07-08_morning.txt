In other news, the autonomous vehicle industry is facing a major federal crackdown. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has issued a direct demand that autonomous vehicle companies stop interfering with first responders at emergency scenes. Apparently, self-driving vehicles have been confused by flashing lights, sirens, and emergency personnel at accident sites, sometimes blocking roads or failing to yield appropriately. The NHTSA made clear that emergency scenes are not "edge cases" that companies can treat as exceptions in their testing protocols. This is a core capability that needs to work perfectly every single time. Emergency response teams depend on being able to reach accident victims as quickly as possible, and autonomous vehicles that obstruct that mission are a genuine public safety hazard. The agency is essentially saying that if your self-driving car can't handle emergency vehicles, it's not ready for public roads. Companies are already scrambling to update their algorithms and emergency response protocols. We'll be watching to see how quickly manufacturers can demonstrate that their vehicles now properly recognize and accommodate first responders. This could delay some rollouts, but it's a necessary safety requirement.
