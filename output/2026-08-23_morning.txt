Speaking of crypto moving into mainstream life, stablecoin spending on crypto cards has crossed a significant milestone. Crypto card spending has now topped one billion dollars as stablecoins are being used for everyday purchases. This is a major shift because it shows people aren't just holding crypto as an investment anymore. They're actually using it at the checkout counter. Stablecoins, which are cryptocurrencies pegged to traditional currencies like the dollar, make this practical because they don't fluctuate wildly in value like Bitcoin or Ethereum. This affects retailers, payment processors, and card issuers who are seeing real transaction volumes. For users, it means the friction of getting in and out of crypto is decreasing. Why this matters is that it demonstrates crypto is becoming a payment rail, not just a speculative asset. As more merchants accept these cards and more people carry crypto balances, it normalizes the technology. Watch for stablecoin payment adoption to accelerate in the coming months, especially as more cards and wallet providers enter the space.

There's also a cautionary note coming from the tokenized stocks space. The CEO of Fairmint is raising an alarm that tokenized stocks could potentially repeat Wall Street's infamous paper crisis from the nineteen sixties. For those not familiar, that was a period when the volume of stock trades overwhelmed the physical and administrative infrastructure of the markets, causing a near-meltdown. The concern with tokenized stocks is that if we create a fractionalized, blockchainbased system without proper settlement infrastructure, we could create similar bottlenecks in the digital era. This matters because tokenized stocks are supposed to make financial markets more efficient and accessible, but if we build them carelessly, we might recreate old problems in new forms. The takeaway is that as this market develops, there needs to be serious infrastructure planning and regulatory oversight. We're still in the early stages of tokenized assets, so now's the time to learn from history.
