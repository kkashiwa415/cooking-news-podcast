On the artificial intelligence front, there's growing criticism about how we think and talk about AI safety. The conventional conversation tends to focus on catastrophic failure scenarios—chatbots encouraging harmful behavior, platforms enabling illegal activity—and while those safeguards absolutely matter, there's an argument that we're missing a broader picture. When companies celebrate because they've built in protections against the worst-case scenarios, we can sometimes overlook everyday harms that are baked into the products themselves. Think about recommendation algorithms steering people toward extreme content, or predictive systems that reinforce biases in hiring and lending. These aren't dramatic disasters that make headlines, but they're systemic issues that affect millions of people in their daily lives. The concern is that by focusing the policy conversation on preventing catastrophic AI failures, we're letting companies off the hook for incremental harms that are happening right now. This matters because it shapes how we regulate AI and what kinds of accountability mechanisms we build. The outlook here is that we're likely to see more friction between the AI industry and policymakers who want to address these everyday harms, not just the doomsday scenarios. Expect more scrutiny of recommendation systems, bias audits, and algorithmic transparency in the months ahead.
