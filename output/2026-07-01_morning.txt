An Italian startup that's been quietly buying up broken internet icons is now worth eighteen billion dollars.

Bending Spoons made its debut on the public markets today with a bang, surging forty percent on its first day of trading. The company's track record speaks for itself — they've acquired and completely revamped a string of beloved but struggling brands. AOL, Eventbrite, Evernote, Meetup, and Vimeo all found new life under Bending Spoons' ownership. The IPO valued the company at eighteen billion dollars, which is a remarkable achievement given that most software-as-a-service companies have been struggling in the current market environment.

What makes Bending Spoons' story even more interesting is the philosophy behind their success. The company's founders learned their lessons the hard way. They've been through startup failure themselves, and that experience shapes everything they do now. In interviews following the IPO, they're pointing to a philosophy that might sound counterintuitive: success comes from minimizing luck. Rather than relying on being in the right place at the right time, Bending Spoons focuses on systematic processes, disciplined acquisition strategies, and a methodical approach to turning around failing products. They're essentially betting that if you remove as much randomness as possible from the equation, you dramatically increase your odds of winning. Today's surge suggests investors are buying into that thesis.

The company's strategy is particularly interesting because it swims against the tide of how Silicon Valley typically operates. Most of the tech industry is obsessed with building the next new thing from scratch. Bending Spoons is doing something different — they're finding products that have lost their way and proving they can be resurrected with the right management and focus. It's a reminder that in tech, disruption isn't always about invention. Sometimes it's about resurrection.

In other news, the venture capital world is seeing some notable shake-ups. Ashton Kutcher is leaving Sound Ventures to launch a brand new fund with Morgan Beller. Sound Ventures built its reputation on making big, concentrated bets on AI labs and category-leading artificial intelligence companies. But Kutcher's new venture appears to be taking a different angle. Instead of betting on the AI companies themselves, this fund is looking at the infrastructure and energy that powers those AI systems. That's a fascinating layer of the stack to focus on, especially as AI companies are burning through enormous amounts of power trying to train and run their models. The infrastructure that supports AI could be just as valuable as the software on top of it.

Speaking of power and infrastructure, let's turn to the cryptocurrency world, where there's been some significant movement today. Ethereum is launching a new institutional push, and it's drawing support from across the Ethereum ecosystem. At the same time, the Ethereum Foundation has laid out a policy guide specifically targeting governments and institutions, outlining use cases for how blockchain technology could be applied at scale. This represents a more mature phase for Ethereum — moving beyond the retail speculation that dominated the earlier years and positioning itself as infrastructure for serious institutional players.

Robinhood is making its own crypto play. The trading platform has rolled out a public blockchain as it expands deeper into cryptocurrency. Robinhood has been gradually moving in this direction for a while now, but a full blockchain launch signals serious commitment to becoming more than just a trading venue. They're positioning themselves as an infrastructure player in the crypto space.

Not all crypto news is positive, though. Jefferies has issued a warning against buying the dip in Circle, one of the major stablecoin issuers, as a new competitor called Open USD raises fresh concerns about competition in the space. The stablecoin market is consolidating, and it's becoming clear that not all stablecoin companies are going to survive the shakeout. Circle faces real headwinds from better-capitalized competitors.

On the bitcoin trading front, analysts at Cantor are suggesting that the bitcoin bear market might be entering its final stretch. That's not quite a bullish call, but it's also not a bearish one. It's more of a "maybe we're close to turning the corner" kind of observation. If that proves accurate, it could signal the beginning of a recovery in crypto assets more broadly.

The flip side of institutional adoption and legitimate crypto infrastructure is the reminder that fraud is still very much a problem in the space. The CEO of Goliath Ventures has pleaded guilty in a four hundred million dollar crypto Ponzi scheme. It's a stark reminder that despite all the talk of institutional money flowing into crypto and blockchain becoming mainstream infrastructure, the space still attracts bad actors who are willing to defraud investors. Those cases serve as important cautionary tales.

Now let's turn to some security and privacy concerns in the tech industry. Apple's Hide My Email feature, which is supposed to give users better privacy by generating temporary email addresses, has a significant bug. According to security researchers, the feature is exposing actual email addresses instead of keeping them hidden. That's a pretty serious problem given the whole purpose of the feature. Apple's been working on privacy features for years, and they've made it a central part of their marketing and user value proposition. A vulnerability like this is particularly embarrassing because it goes to the core of what the feature is supposed to do.

Over at Meta, WhatsApp has rolled out a new usernames feature, and it's already raising red flags about impersonation. Meta says the feature improves privacy, but critics and security researchers are questioning whether the safeguards are robust enough to prevent people from impersonating others. The irony here is that Meta is trying to add a privacy feature, but the implementation might create new security vulnerabilities. It's a reminder that in tech, adding new features without thinking through all the ways they can be misused is a common pitfall.

Finally, SpaceX is showing investors what appears to be an early prototype of an AI device that sounds remarkably phone-like. This is the latest signal that SpaceX might be expanding into the wireless and consumer devices space. Elon Musk has been talking about moving beyond rockets and satellites for a while now, and a handheld AI device would be a natural extension of SpaceX's ambitions. If SpaceX does launch a consumer device, it would be another example of Musk's companies blurring the lines between different industries — mixing aerospace, energy, neural interfaces, and now potentially consumer electronics.
