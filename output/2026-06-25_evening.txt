IBM just made a move that could reshape computing for the next decade.

The company's unveiled a prototype chip packing around one hundred billion transistors into an area the size of a fingernail—that's twice the density of anything they've pulled off before, going all the way back to their previous breakthrough in twenty twenty-one. We're talking sub-one nanometer technology here, which is a pretty massive leap forward. The design uses a completely new approach to stacking transistors and routing power through the chip, which lets them squeeze more processing power into less space while actually using less energy to do it. This matters because for the last fifty years, the semiconductor industry has been chasing Moore's Law—this idea that you can keep doubling transistor density every couple years. Most experts thought we were hitting a wall. IBM's prototype suggests we might have another decade or more of runway left. The real test comes next though. Moving from a laboratory prototype to actual mass production is always the hardest part, so we'll be watching to see if they can scale this up and when we might actually see these chips in real devices.
