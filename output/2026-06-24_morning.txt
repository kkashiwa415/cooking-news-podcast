Shifting gears to cybersecurity and authentication. There's a new website out there that's naming and shaming the major companies that still don't offer passkeys to their users. Twenty-four percent of the most popular websites in the world don't support passkeys, which are widely considered the most secure way to log into apps and services. Passkeys replace passwords entirely and are basically unhackable using the methods that work on traditional credentials. The fact that a quarter of major websites still haven't implemented them suggests we're still in the early innings of this transition.

Why it matters is that passkeys are the future of authentication. They're more secure, they're easier for users, and they eliminate entire categories of attacks. But adoption requires companies to build new infrastructure and update their sign-in systems. Some companies have been slow to prioritize this, and the new website is basically calling them out publicly. Expect more pressure on these laggards, especially as security breaches continue to make headlines and users become more aware of passkeys as an option.
