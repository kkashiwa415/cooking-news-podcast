California's about to make streaming services turn down the volume on those obnoxiously loud ads, and it's happening in just three days.

A new California law takes effect on July first that targets the audio levels in streaming advertisements. If you've ever been startled awake by a commercial that's somehow twice as loud as the show you were watching, you know exactly how annoying this is. The regulation requires that ads meet the same audio standards as the programs they're inserted into, which means they can't just blast through your speakers at maximum volume to grab your attention.

This law has been pushed by consumer advocates frustrated with the disconnect between content and advertising volume. Streamers like Netflix, Disney Plus, Hulu, and others are the ones who'll need to enforce this on their platforms. The rule applies across the board to any streaming service operating in California, which is basically all of them given the state's massive population and spending power.

For viewers, this is a quality of life improvement. No more jumping at the TV because an ad came roaring in during a quiet scene. For advertisers, they'll need to be a bit more creative about capturing attention without relying on audio shock value. For streaming platforms, they'll need to implement stronger audio normalization systems to keep advertisers in compliance. This matters because it's the first major regulation of its kind in the streaming age, and other states are likely watching to see how it works out. If California succeeds in turning down the volume without crushing ad effectiveness, expect similar laws to roll out nationwide.

For investors watching semiconductor stocks, Micron deserves a closer look. The company's earnings visibility is strong given the multiyear buildout of AI infrastructure across the industry.
