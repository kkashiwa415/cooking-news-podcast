Crusoe Energy's bold plan to power its AI data centers with cutting-edge supersonic jet engines just got shelved.

Boom's CEO Blake Scholl confirmed that the technology simply isn't ready for Crusoe's immediate roadmap. This is a significant setback for both companies, which had positioned themselves at the intersection of AI's explosive power demands and next-generation energy innovation. Crusoe's data centers require massive amounts of electricity to run their AI infrastructure, and the company had seen Boom's compact, efficient turbines as a potential game-changer for meeting those needs. The broader AI industry is watching these kinds of partnerships closely because the sector's power consumption is becoming a genuine constraint on growth. Without innovative solutions like this, companies are forced back to traditional power grids that are already straining under demand. Keep an eye on whether Crusoe finds alternative approaches or if Boom can accelerate its timeline for other industrial partners.

A major cryptocurrency heist has led to swift action from stablecoin issuers. Circle and Tether have stepped in to freeze the hacker's wallet following a massive theft from Bitget, one of the larger crypto exchanges. This is a significant moment because it shows how stablecoin issuers wield real power in the crypto ecosystem. When they freeze accounts, stolen funds effectively become unreachable, which can actually deter theft and make exchanges more secure. The Bitget heist was substantial enough to warrant immediate action from two of the biggest players in stablecoins, which tells you the scale of what was stolen. For the users who lost funds, having Circle and Tether move quickly is genuinely helpful, though it doesn't recover the money—it just makes it harder for the thieves to convert their haul into real value. This matters because it demonstrates that the crypto ecosystem does have some built-in circuit breakers, even if they're administered by centralized entities. Moving forward, expect exchanges to invest more heavily in security, and expect hackers to look for ways to move stolen assets through channels that bypass stablecoin freezes.
