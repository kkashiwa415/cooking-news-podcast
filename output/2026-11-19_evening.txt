In other news, turning to biomedical breakthroughs, Australian researchers just achieved something remarkable. They've kept a human liver alive outside the body for a record seven weeks on life support. The feat was led by surgeon and inventor Carlo Pulitano and his team in Sydney, connecting a donated liver to specialized equipment that essentially kept it functioning as if it were still inside a person. The details are still pending publication, but the implications are genuinely significant. This opens up possibilities that were previously thought impossible—the idea that organs could be maintained, repaired, transported, and potentially even improved while outside the body. That matters enormously for patients waiting on transplant lists. Right now, the window for getting an organ from donor to recipient is incredibly narrow, usually just hours before the organ starts to deteriorate. If livers can be kept viable for weeks, it fundamentally changes the logistics of organ transplantation. It gives surgeons more time to match organs with the right patients, potentially improves outcomes by allowing time for treatment or repair, and could dramatically increase the effective organ supply without needing more donors. Watch for this research to be published formally soon, and for other teams around the world to try replicating these results with other organs. This could genuinely save thousands of lives.
